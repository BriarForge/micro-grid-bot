# Iteration v2: adaptive compounding BTC-USDT grid

## Headline balance

The best tested v2 candidate, a **40% close-based two-sided recenter threshold**, turned
**10,000 USDT into 13,989.525537–14,044.912293 USDT** from 2022-08-13 through 2025-10-07.
That is a total return of **39.8953%–40.4491%**, or approximately **11.23%–11.37% annualized**.
Both minute-path simulations ended entirely in USDT, so the reported balance contains no unsold
BTC or unrealized BTC value.

This improves substantially on iteration v1's 10,556.126729–10,570.503421 USDT, but still trails
the fee-adjusted BTC buy-and-hold benchmark of 49,681.834559 USDT.

The minimum equity was about 22.7% below the starting balance. Fees consumed approximately 47.8%
of gross grid-cycle profit, so execution quality remains a major risk even though the rebalance
itself was inexpensive.

## Improvements implemented in the simulation

1. **Two-sided adaptive recentering.** At a completed minute close 40% above or below the current
   center, all simulated grid orders are canceled and rebuilt around that close. The rule is causal
   and does not inspect future prices.
2. **Inventory rebalance.** Each rebuild buys or sells only enough BTC to fund the new 13-order sell
   ladder. The 12 buy levels and permanent 35% reserve remain funded.
3. **Full-equity compounding.** Every completed sell sizes its replacement buy as
   `current equity × 65% ÷ 25`; a recenter rebuilds all 25 levels from current equity.
4. **Fee-complete reset accounting.** Maker fees are charged on grid fills and taker fees on BTC
   inventory rebalances.
5. **Risk constraints.** The existing 65% maximum BTC exposure and 60% resume hysteresis remain in
   force. Neither path used negative cash or exceeded the exposure ceiling.

Research on dynamic grid strategies likewise identifies dynamic grid resetting as the key change
from a traditional static grid. For live OKX execution, post-only orders that would immediately
match are canceled, and cancellation acknowledgments are not final until the private order stream
reports the canceled state; the production engine must handle both behaviors.

The sharp 40% peak is a material overfitting warning. This threshold was selected using the same
history on which its performance is reported. It should be treated as a candidate for walk-forward
testing, not a proven production parameter. Frequent resetting was catastrophic at the account's
0.08% maker fee because it repeatedly repositioned the tight grid and generated excessive turnover.

## Remaining production requirements

- Recenter only after every cancel is confirmed through OKX private order updates; cancel requests
  and batch acknowledgments alone are insufficient.
- Retry post-only orders canceled for crossing the book without converting them silently to taker
  orders.
- Reserve balances for all open buys and sells, reconcile partial fills during a rebuild, and make
  each recenter restart-safe and idempotent.
- Add turnover/fee budgets and a recenter cooldown so abnormal volatility cannot create an order
  storm.
- Validate the 40% candidate with walk-forward windows, demo trading, realistic queue fills,
  spread/slippage, API failures, and cancellation races.
